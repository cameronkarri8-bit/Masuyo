**Short answer:** most UK small business websites cost between £1,000 and £15,000 to build. A simple brochure site sits at the lower end. A custom build with bookings, memberships or integrations sits at the upper end. Four things move the number: page count, how custom the design is, what the site has to do, and who builds it.

## What actually drives the cost

Web design quotes look wildly inconsistent until you understand what is being priced. It is almost never the same thing twice.

**Page count.** Every page needs layout, copy and testing. A five-page site is genuinely half the work of a twelve-page site, which is why page count is the first question any decent quote will ask about.

**Design approach.** Buying a template and adjusting the colours is fast. Designing something specific to your business takes days of work and shows up directly in the price.

**Functionality.** A contact form is trivial. Online booking, member logins, payment handling, a course platform or a CRM integration each add real engineering time and ongoing responsibility.

**Who builds it.** A student, a freelancer, a small studio and a twenty-person agency all charge differently for the same brief. The gap is mostly overhead and process, not always skill.

Most small businesses that take their website seriously land in the £2,500 to £8,000 band. If you are being quoted £500 for something that sounds like it belongs in the £6,000 band, the scope is different to what you think it is.

## What the headline price usually excludes

This is where budgets get blown, so it is worth checking every quote against this list.

- Copywriting, which is often quoted as "client to supply"
- Photography and any licensed imagery
- Ongoing hosting, domain renewal and SSL
- Third-party software licences for booking or membership systems
- Post-launch changes beyond an agreed revision round
- SEO work beyond basic technical setup

None of these are hidden fees when they are stated upfront. They become a problem when a quote is silent on them and you discover them at launch.

## The ongoing cost nobody quotes for

A website is not a one-off purchase. It needs hosting, updates, backups and someone to fix it when something breaks.

For a small business site, expect £150 to £1,200 a year depending on how much is handled for you. We have broken this down properly in our guide to [website care plans](/guides/website-care-plans), including what should and should not be inside one.

The platform you build on affects this figure more than most people expect. WordPress sites carry ongoing plugin and security maintenance that modern static builds largely avoid, which we cover in [Next.js vs WordPress](/guides/nextjs-vs-wordpress).

## Where cheap gets expensive

Low-cost builds are not a scam. They are a scope decision, and the trade-off is usually the same three things.

You get a template rather than a design built around how your customers actually buy. You get filler copy rather than words written to convert. You get no measurement, so nobody can tell you whether the site is working.

The result is a site that exists but does not earn. If you want the site to produce enquiries rather than just look tidy, start with [what a small business website should include](/guides/small-business-website-checklist) and work backwards to a budget from there.

## How to compare quotes properly

Price is the last thing to compare, not the first. Line up the quotes and check they agree on four things before you look at the numbers.

1. Page count and which pages
2. Who is writing the copy
3. What happens after launch, and for how long
4. Who owns the code, the domain and the hosting account

If two quotes differ on any of those, you are not comparing like for like. Writing a clear brief before you approach anyone solves most of this, and we have set out how in [how to brief a web design agency](/guides/how-to-brief-a-web-design-agency).
