**Short answer:** a small business website needs five to eight pages, one clear action per page, proof that you are real and competent, and a fast mobile experience. Everything else is optional. Most sites fail not because something is missing but because there is too much competing for attention.

## The pages you actually need

Start here and add only what your business genuinely requires.

**Home page.** Says what you do, who for, and what to do next. Someone should understand your business within five seconds of landing.

**A page per core service.** One page per thing you sell, not one page listing everything. Service pages are what rank in search and what people read before they enquire.

**About page.** For small businesses this is often the second most visited page. People are checking whether you are real, local and trustworthy.

**Contact page.** A form, an email address, a phone number if you take calls, and a location if it matters. Do not make people hunt.

**Proof page.** Case studies, reviews, results or client logos. Whatever form it takes, you need something that shows the work rather than describing it.

## What every page needs

The structure matters more than the volume of content.

- A single clear heading that says what the page is about
- One primary action, repeated rather than competing with three others
- Proof close to the point of decision, not buried on a separate page
- Copy written for a customer rather than for a search engine

We cap ourselves at two calls to action across an entire site. More than that and every option gets weaker.

## The technical basics

None of this is optional, and all of it is invisible until it is missing.

**Speed.** Slow sites lose enquiries. Aim for a page that becomes usable in under two seconds on a mid-range phone on 4G, not on your office fibre connection.

**Mobile first.** Design for the smallest screen and let it scale up. Testing on a desktop browser window that has been dragged narrow is not the same as testing on a phone.

**HTTPS and a working SSL certificate.** Browsers now flag sites without one, which is the fastest possible way to lose trust.

**Analytics.** If you cannot see what people do on your site, you cannot improve it. Privacy-friendly options like Umami or Plausible avoid cookie banner complexity entirely.

**Structured data.** Schema markup helps search engines and AI answer engines understand what your business is and does. It is a small job that pays off for years.

## The forms question

Most small business sites lose enquiries at the form, not before it.

Ask for the minimum you need to have a useful first conversation. Name, email and a message will do for most businesses. Every additional field reduces the number of people who finish.

Then make sure the form actually works. Send yourself a test enquiry monthly, and check it is not landing in spam. Broken forms are the single most common problem we find when auditing existing sites.

## What you can skip

Plenty of standard website features add cost and clutter without adding enquiries.

- A carousel or slider on the home page
- A blog you will not maintain
- A live chat widget nobody is monitoring
- Stock photography of people in offices who are not your team
- A "our process" page longer than the service page it supports

If you cannot explain what a page or feature is for in one sentence, it probably does not belong.
